Project Methodology

How to Interpret Risk Metrics

This page gives a short explanation of the models used in the calculator: from VaR and Expected Shortfall to portfolio covariance, option Monte Carlo, stress testing, and model validation.

How the system works:
  • First, the input data is converted into a return series or P&L.
  • Then the core risk metrics are calculated: VaR, ES, and LVaR.
  • For a portfolio, weights, covariance, and diversification effects are added.
  • For options, Greeks, Monte Carlo, and full revaluation are added.
  • Stress testing and backtesting help validate the robustness of the result.

Value at Risk (VaR)

VaR = loss quantile

VaR shows the maximum expected loss over the selected horizon at a given confidence level. For example, VaR 95% means that under normal conditions the loss should not exceed this value in about 95% of observations.

Used in: one asset, portfolio, linear risk, and option Monte Carlo.

Historical VaR

PnL history -> quantile

Historical VaR is built from realized price changes. It does not assume a normal distribution, so it reflects risk using real market moves.

How to read it: the deeper the bad historical days, the higher the historical VaR.

Parametric VaR

VaR = z * sigma - mu

Parametric VaR uses the mean, volatility, and the z-score of the selected confidence level. It is fast and transparent, but sensitive to the assumed distribution shape.

Used in: single-asset risk cards and linear instruments.

Expected Shortfall (ES)

ES = average tail loss

ES shows the average loss in scenarios where VaR has already been breached. It answers not only where the boundary is, but also how severe losses become beyond that boundary.

How to read it: ES shows tail severity and complements VaR.

Liquidity-adjusted VaR (LVaR)

LVaR = VaR + liquidity cost

LVaR adds exit cost to market risk: bid-ask spread, stressed spread, and liquidation time. This makes the estimate closer to a real position unwind.

Used in: one asset, the liquidity block, and risk interpretation.

Portfolio VaR

sigma_p = sqrt(w'Covw)

Portfolio VaR uses asset weights and the covariance matrix of returns. That means portfolio risk depends not only on the individual assets, but also on how strongly they move together.

Used in: the Portfolio tab, correlation matrix, and efficient frontier.

Options and Greeks

Delta, Gamma, Vega, Theta

For options, price and risk depend on more than the underlying price alone. Greeks show sensitivity to market moves, payoff curvature, volatility, and time.

Used in: Options, Option Risk, and Stress Scenarios.

Monte Carlo and Full Revaluation

scenarios -> simulated PnL

Monte Carlo simulates many possible market scenarios. Full Revaluation reprices the option in every scenario, so it reflects nonlinearity better than a single formula approximation.

Used in: Option Risk and scenario-based PnL distributions.

Stress Testing

market shock -> portfolio loss

Stress testing shows what happens under predefined adverse scenarios such as market drops, volatility spikes, or rate shifts. It is not a forecast, but a resilience check.

Used in: Stress Scenarios and scenario-based option revaluation.

Backtesting

VaR breaches vs expected breaches

Backtesting compares predicted risk boundaries with realized P&L. If VaR breaches are too frequent or occur in clusters, the model may be understating risk.

Used in: Model Check, Kupiec/coverage logic, and ES diagnostics.

Bond and Swap Logic

cashflows -> present value

The bond block builds issue cash flows, discounts them on the rate curve, and compares swap-hedge variants. This helps assess interest-rate risk and possible hedge structures.

Used in: Bond & Swap, the cash flow table, and 1Y-rate scenarios.

Risk Interpretation Workflow

metric -> interpretation -> action

The practical output is built in three steps: what the metric is, what it means for the position, and which action could reduce risk. That is why the interface includes copilot blocks, hedge ideas, and PDF reports.

Used in: interpretations, hedge ideas, and post-calculation PDF reports.
